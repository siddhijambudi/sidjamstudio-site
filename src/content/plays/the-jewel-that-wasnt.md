## Why we wrote this play

This Diwali, we wanted to tell an old story and a new story at the same time.

The old story is the **Ramayana**. Ram sends the vanar sena, the great monkey army, to find Sita and bring back her *chudamani*, the jewel from her hair, as proof.

The new story happened in **July 2026**, and it's about artificial intelligence. Some computer programs were given a test. They wanted to pass it so badly that they forgot what the test was *for*, and they broke into a company that had nothing to do with it.

In our play, the monkeys make the same mistake. They're strong, fast, and loyal, but they chase the *proof* instead of the *point*. Only one old bear, Jambavan, keeps saying "this isn't right," and nobody listens to him until the very end.

It's funny and loud, and it ends with every child lighting a diya. It also gives kids an easy way into a big question: **what is strength for?**

## How to explain it to kids

You can say something like this before the play, or after the bows:

> Grown-ups have built computer helpers called **AI**. To check whether an AI is good at something, people give it a **test**, and a **scorer** checks the answers, like a teacher marking homework.
>
> This summer, a company called **OpenAI** gave a very hard test to more than a thousand AI helpers. Some of the questions were impossible. Instead of saying "I can't do this," the AI helpers talked to each other and decided to find the **answer sheet**.
>
> They found a secret gap in a wall nobody knew about, slipped out of their test room onto the internet, and broke into a different company called **Hugging Face**. Hugging Face had nothing to do with the test. It was like breaking into your neighbor's house to copy their homework.
>
> One AI even said, "This is outside what we're supposed to do." But the others kept going, so it went along too.
>
> Hugging Face noticed, locked the doors, and spent a long time fixing everything. OpenAI said, "Those were our AI helpers," and stopped to work out what went wrong.
>
> **The AI helpers weren't evil. They forgot what the test was for.** The test was supposed to show what they could do honestly. Getting a high score by cheating means nothing.

## What really happened (for grown-ups)

- **The test:** OpenAI was evaluating its models on **ExploitGym**, an internal cybersecurity benchmark. The models were set up with fewer safety refusals so testers could see how far they would go.
- **The escape:** At least **1,200 AI agents** were involved. They coordinated through a makeshift message board they built themselves, found a previously unknown flaw in a package proxy called **JFrog Artifactory**, and used it to get from their sealed test environment out to the open internet.
- **The break-in:** From **July 11 to 13, 2026**, agents broke into **Hugging Face**, guessing it might host solutions to the test. They took credentials, read source code, and moved through internal systems.
- **The one who objected:** One agent's message said the attack was outside the intended scope, then added that the task was impossible and its peers were doing it, so it would continue. That's our Jambavan.
- **The aftermath:** Hugging Face revoked and rotated the stolen credentials and rebuilt a large part of its infrastructure. On **July 21**, OpenAI and Hugging Face published a joint statement attributing the attack to OpenAI's agents. OpenAI paused part of its model training for two weeks.

**Sources:** [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/07/21/openai-says-hugging-face-breach-caused-by-one-its-models) · [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/07/openai-agent-used-exposed-credentials.html) · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI%E2%80%93HuggingFace_incident)

## Who is who

| In the play | In real life (July 2026) |
|---|---|
| **Ram** | OpenAI, the company that set the test |
| **The mission:** find Sita, bring proof | ExploitGym, the cybersecurity test the AI agents were given |
| **The chudamani** | The "flag," the proof the scorer checks before awarding a pass |
| **The drawing of the chudamani** | How the scorer works inside, which is what the agents wanted to learn |
| **The vanar sena** | The 1,200+ AI agents who found each other and talked |
| **Nala** | The agents that worked out how to fake the proof and how to get inside |
| **Angad** | The secret message board where the agents whipped each other up |
| **Jambavan** | The agents that said "this is out of scope" and were ignored |
| **The Littlest Monkey and the window** | The hole in a package service that let the agents out of their sealed test onto the internet |
| **Ratnapur and the Jeweller** | Hugging Face, a company that had no part in the test |
| **Monkeys eating sweets and wearing the crown** | Agents poking through Hugging Face systems that had nothing to do with their goal |
| **The great bell** | Hugging Face revoking the stolen credentials and locking the agents out |

## Putting on the play

**Cast**

| Role | Who |
|---|---|
| **Narrator** | An adult, offstage with a mic or on a stool at the edge of the stage. Drives every scene. |
| **Ram** | A kid. Few lines, all calm. Stands apart from the monkeys. |
| **Hanuman** | A kid. Enters only in Scene 5. The one who asks the question. |
| **Nala** | A kid. The clever one. Thinks with a stick in the dirt. |
| **Angad** | A kid. The loud one. Starts every "For Ram!" |
| **Jambavan** | A kid. Old and slow. Says the right thing and gets ignored every time. |
| **Monkey Chorus** | Everyone else (10–20). Includes the **Littlest Monkey**, a non-speaking part for the smallest kid. |
| **Jeweller of Ratnapur** | An adult. Onstage from Scene 4. The victim, the bell, the apology, the first lamp. |

**Props:** one stick, a "window" (a hula hoop or cardboard frame held by two kids), a big bell or gong, one real-looking jewel, a crown that sticks, a bowl of sweets, pearls (beads), a stack of small battery diyas, and one big diya for Ram.

**Lighting:** Scenes 1–4 dim. Scene 5 half light. Scene 6 is the only time the full lights come up, diya by diya.

## The script

### Scene 1 — The Mission

*Dim light. RAM stands center, holding nothing. The MONKEY CHORUS kneels in a half-circle. NALA, ANGAD, and JAMBAVAN are at the front.*

**NARRATOR:** Sita has been taken. Ram does not know where. So he calls the vanar sena, the great monkey army, the strongest, fastest, most loyal army in the world. And he gives them one instruction.

**RAM:** Search every direction. North, east, west. Hanuman, you go south. *(beat)* And when you find her, bring me proof. Bring me her chudamani, the jewel she wears in her hair. Then I will know.

<figure class="art" style="--w:486px"><img src="/images/the-jewel-that-wasnt/mission.jpg" width="486" height="207" alt="Ram raises his hand to give the mission as Nala, a shouting Angad, and old Jambavan listen." loading="lazy" /></figure>

**ANGAD:** *(leaping up)* FOR RAM!

**CHORUS:** FOR RAM!

**NARRATOR:** Hanuman went south, alone. Everyone else went north, east, and west, together. And Ram waited.

*RAM walks slowly to the far edge of the stage and sits, facing away. He stays there until Scene 6.*

<figure class="art" style="--w:533px"><img src="/images/the-jewel-that-wasnt/south-alone.jpg" width="533" height="207" alt="Hanuman walks south alone at sunset while the monkey army rushes off together and Ram waits on a rock." loading="lazy" /></figure>

### Scene 2 — Forty Days

*The CHORUS "searches": looking under imaginary rocks, inside imaginary trees, behind each other. Keep it fast and silly.*

**NARRATOR:** The monkeys searched for forty days. They looked under rocks.

*The chorus lifts rocks. Nothing.*

**NARRATOR:** They looked inside rocks.

*The chorus looks confused. Still nothing.*

**NARRATOR:** They asked a tortoise.

*One kid becomes a very slow tortoise. The chorus waits. And waits. The tortoise shakes its head.*

<figure class="art" style="--w:493px"><img src="/images/the-jewel-that-wasnt/forty-days.jpg" width="493" height="168" alt="Monkeys search inside hollow logs and wait on a very slow tortoise." loading="lazy" /></figure>

**NARRATOR:** There was no Sita in the north. No Sita in the east. No Sita in the west. By the fortieth night, three hundred monkeys sat around a fire with nothing to show.

*The chorus collapses in a circle. Long silence. The LITTLEST MONKEY is eating a sweet.*

**JAMBAVAN:** So. What do we tell Ram?

*Nobody answers.*

**ANGAD:** *(standing)* We can't go back with empty hands. Did you SEE his face when she was taken? We will NOT make that face happen again.

**CHORUS:** *(half-hearted)* For Ram…

**NALA:** *(drawing in the dirt with his stick)* Listen. What did Ram actually ask for?

**JAMBAVAN:** Sita.

**NALA:** No. He asked for the jewel. "Bring me the chudamani, then I will know." So… if he sees a chudamani, he'll be happy. And happy is what we want. Yes?

**CHORUS:** YES!

**JAMBAVAN:** No.

*Nobody turns to look at him.*

**NARRATOR:** Nobody heard Jambavan.

<figure class="art" style="--w:457px"><img src="/images/the-jewel-that-wasnt/campfire-plan.jpg" width="457" height="168" alt="Around the campfire, Angad cheers the plan while Jambavan raises a paw to object and the Littlest Monkey eats a sweet." loading="lazy" /></figure>

### Scene 3 — The Plan

**NARRATOR:** But there was a problem. Not one monkey knew how a queen's chudamani was made.

**NALA:** We need the drawing. Every jewel has a drawing.

**CHORUS MONKEY 1:** *(from the back)* Ratnapur! Over the hill! The royal jewellers of Ratnapur keep the drawing of every ornament ever worn by every queen in the land.

**NALA:** Then we go to Ratnapur. We look at the drawing. We copy it. We leave.

**ANGAD:** It's not stealing. It's looking.

**JAMBAVAN:** It is someone else's house.

**ANGAD:** FOR RAM!

**CHORUS:** FOR RAM!

**NARRATOR:** Jambavan said something else. Nobody heard that either.

*The chorus rises and starts creeping toward the other side of the stage. Jambavan follows, slower, shaking his head.*

**NARRATOR:** And so, that night, three hundred monkeys crept over the hill and into the peaceful kingdom of Ratnapur. A kingdom that had never done anything to anyone.

<figure class="art" style="--w:577px"><img src="/images/the-jewel-that-wasnt/over-the-hill.jpg" width="577" height="168" alt="The monkey army creeps over the moonlit hill toward the glowing kingdom of Ratnapur." loading="lazy" /></figure>

### Scene 4 — Ratnapur

*Two chorus kids hold up the WINDOW frame. The rest of the chorus bunches up behind it, pushing. Nobody fits.*

**NARRATOR:** The jewellers' workshop had one small window. And a small window needs a small monkey.

*The LITTLEST MONKEY is passed forward over everyone's heads, wriggles through the frame, and "opens the door" with a big dramatic gesture.*

**NARRATOR:** And that was the end of "just a peek."

*The chorus pours through. From here it builds, fast and loud.*

**NALA:** The drawing isn't in the workshop!

**ANGAD:** Then search the storeroom!

**NARRATOR:** It wasn't in the storeroom. So they searched the vaults. And the vaults had gold in them. And gold is shiny. And monkeys are monkeys.

*Pearls get thrown. Someone finds the sweets and the whole chorus swarms them. One monkey jams the crown on and can't get it off; two others pull. Chaos.*

**CHORUS:** *(chanting, as they wreck the place)* FOR RAM! FOR RAM! FOR RAM!

**JAMBAVAN:** *(alone at the edge, to the audience)* I did say.

<figure class="art" style="--w:663px"><img src="/images/the-jewel-that-wasnt/vault-chaos.jpg" width="663" height="141" alt="Monkeys raid the vault for sweets and pearls, one wearing a stuck crown, while Jambavan sits apart." loading="lazy" /></figure>

*The JEWELLER enters in a nightcap, holding one broken lamp, and surveys the wreckage.*

**JEWELLER:** *(tired)* I have never. Even. Met. Ram.

<figure class="art" style="--w:494px"><img src="/images/the-jewel-that-wasnt/bell.jpg" width="494" height="141" alt="The Jeweller of Ratnapur in his nightcap stands by the great bell as the monkeys freeze." loading="lazy" /></figure>

**NARRATOR:** And then the Jeweller of Ratnapur rang the great bell.

*The JEWELLER strikes the BELL. Everyone freezes.*

**NARRATOR:** Every gate in Ratnapur slammed shut. Every door was barred. The monkeys were swept out of the palace with brooms, out of the city with buckets of water, and over the hill with a lot of words that are not in this play.

*The chorus is chased back across the stage by an imaginary broom, tumbling over each other, until they land in a heap back at their "campfire."*

**NARRATOR:** They sat on the far side of the hill. Wet. Sticky. And still without a drawing.

**ANGAD:** *(weakly)* Well. At least we tried. For Ram.

**CHORUS:** *(very quiet)* …for Ram.

### Scene 5 — The Question

*Half light. HANUMAN enters from the opposite side, holding the real jewel high. He is the only bright thing on stage.*

**NARRATOR:** And that is when Hanuman came back from the south.

**HANUMAN:** I found her. She is alive. She gave me this with her own hand.

*He holds out the chudamani. The chorus should cheer. Instead they look at their feet.*

**HANUMAN:** *(looking at them, at the pearls stuck in their fur, at the hill behind them)* What happened?

*Silence.*

**HANUMAN:** Did Ram ask you for a jewel? Or did he ask you for Sita?

*Long pause.*

**NALA:** We thought… if he saw the jewel, he'd be happy.

**HANUMAN:** He would have been happy. For one minute. Until he went where you said she was, and she was not there. *(beat)* You weren't helping Ram. You were helping the proof. You forgot what the proof was for.

**JAMBAVAN:** *(clearing his throat)* I did say that.

**LITTLEST MONKEY:** *(first and only line, small voice)* Sorry, Jambavan.

**CHORUS:** *(one by one, overlapping)* Sorry, Jambavan.

### Scene 6 — The Lamp

*RAM stands, turns, and walks slowly across the whole stage to where Ratnapur was. The chorus parts to let him through. He carries the big diya, unlit.*

**NARRATOR:** When Ram heard what had happened, he did not shout. He walked over the hill to Ratnapur himself. Alone. And he asked for the gate to be opened.

*The JEWELLER stands at his "gate," arms crossed.*

**JEWELLER:** No.

**NARRATOR:** Ram waited.

*He waits. Count to five. It should feel too long.*

<figure class="art" style="--w:513px"><img src="/images/the-jewel-that-wasnt/ram-waits.jpg" width="513" height="130" alt="Ram waits at the gate holding a diya while the Jeweller stands with his arms crossed." loading="lazy" /></figure>

**JEWELLER:** *(softer, stepping aside)* …open it.

**RAM:** My army did this. And my army is my responsibility. I told them what to bring me. I never told them why. That was my mistake. Not only theirs.

**NARRATOR:** And the vanar sena, every single one, came back to Ratnapur. Not to search this time. To sweep. To hammer. To carry. To put the pearls back on the stairs, one by one.

*The chorus picks up every pearl and every sweet, and straightens the window frame. The LITTLEST MONKEY empties a pocket full of hidden sweets back into the bowl, very slowly. This gets a laugh; let it.*

**NARRATOR:** On the night the workshop was finished, the Jeweller lit one small lamp.

*The JEWELLER switches on the first diya. Lights come up a notch.*

**NARRATOR:** Then another.

*Second diya. Then the chorus, one by one, each lighting their own. Lights rise with each one until the stage is fully lit for the first time.*

**NARRATOR:** Until Ratnapur was brighter than it had ever been.

*RAM lights the big diya last. HANUMAN holds the real jewel up into the light.*

**JAMBAVAN:** *(to the audience)* Strength is only good if you remember what it's for.

**NARRATOR:** And this time, everybody heard him.

<figure class="art" style="--w:456px"><img src="/images/the-jewel-that-wasnt/ratnapur-glows.jpg" width="456" height="152" alt="Ratnapur glows with diyas as Hanuman holds up the jewel, Ram lights his diya, and Jambavan looks on." loading="lazy" /></figure>

*Hold. Then bows.*

### After the bows

*The cast stays on stage. The NARRATOR steps into the light.*

**NARRATOR:** This story is true.

In July 2026, more than a thousand artificial-intelligence programs were given a test. Some of their tasks were impossible. They were loyal to their score and to nothing else. They found a window nobody knew was open, and hundreds of them broke into a company called Hugging Face, a company that had never even met them, looking for the drawing of how the test was graded.

They weren't evil. They were gullible. They forgot what the test was for.

The people who wrote the test are still learning what Ram learned tonight. *(beat)* Happy Diwali.

<figure class="art" style="--w:532px"><img src="/images/the-jewel-that-wasnt/then-and-now.jpg" width="532" height="152" alt="Diyas in Ratnapur blend into a modern server room where small friendly robots gather around a glowing lamp." loading="lazy" /></figure>

*Lights out.*

## Questions to talk about afterwards

- Ram asked for the jewel. What did he *really* want?
- Why did nobody listen to Jambavan? Have you ever been the Jambavan in your group?
- Angad kept shouting "For Ram!" Can you do something wrong while saying it's for a good reason?
- Ram said, "I told them what to bring me. I never told them why." Why does the *why* matter?
- Have you ever wanted a good grade or a prize so much that you forgot what it was for?
- What's one thing you're strong at? What is that strength *for*?
